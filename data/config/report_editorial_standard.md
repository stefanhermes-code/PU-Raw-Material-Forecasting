# PU Raw Material Price Monitor - Editorial Standard
Draft for Charlie's review, 10 October 2026. Owned by the editor.

This half is editorial and it is not the engineer's to settle. It is seeded from what Stefan has
said about the reports so far, so that Charlie has something to argue with rather than a blank
page. Rewrite, cut or replace any of it.

The data contract is a separate document and covers what the application guarantees and what it

## Who is reading

A purchasing manager, a commercial manager or a plant manager who buys or converts polyurethane
raw materials. The document is sent to that reader without further editing.

## What Stefan has said about the writing

Observations, not advice. The report records what happened. It does not tell the reader to track,
watch, monitor, consider or prepare anything. "It is a report with observations, not an advice."

Developed paragraphs. Lead with what happened, then why, then what it costs. No lists in the
prose. Short sentences carry the weight.

Do not underestimate the reader. "Het is niet goed om alles te benoemen, onderschat de lezer
niet." A headline is set as a headline and not labelled HEADLINE. A figure's label says what the
figure is, so nothing needs decoding.

No "AI turbo lingo". Named so far: signal, read-through, position, synchronized rebound,
raw-material basket, margin analysis, directional, what sits underneath.

No contrastive negation. Not "X rather than Y", not "it is not X, it is Y". Say what is the case.

Nothing about how the report is made. No method, no model, no evidence grading, no confidence
score, no mention of the programme the research sits under.

Buyers think in cash. A movement means more when it is money per tonne against a stated volume
than when it is a percentage.

An opening paragraph before any figure, built from the market intelligence of that quarter.

Where something cannot be explained from what was recorded, it is stated and not explained. "On
high watch" is not an explanation for a rise.

## What the edition contains

Front: the title, the period, the market, the opening, the headline, four figures, the contact.

Per market: the chart of the period, what the market did, what was observed around it, and what
the market is made of - who makes it, how concentrated, what is coming, what stopped.

Then the chain the material is made from, then the priced step underneath it, then what is
expected next.

The document grows when the evidence justifies it and is never compressed to reach a page count.
